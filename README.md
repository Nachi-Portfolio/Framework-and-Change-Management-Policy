# Solstice Health Analytics: Policy Framework and Change Management Policy

This project establishes a foundational **Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)** policy framework for Solstice Health Analytics. It defines essential information security policies and a structured change management process to reduce operational risks, protect patient wellness data, and maintain secure and stable technology environments.

## Project Overview

The purpose of this project is to develop a policy framework and formal change management policy for Solstice Health Analytics, a healthcare analytics organization that manages patient wellness data and supporting technology systems.

The project focuses on establishing clear governance requirements, controlling system changes, reducing unauthorized modifications, and protecting sensitive information.

**Project Objectives:**

- Establish four foundational information security policies and two advanced policies.
- Define policy hierarchy, ownership, approval authority, and review requirements.
- Implement a structured change management lifecycle.
- Ensure changes are formally requested, assessed, approved, implemented, and reviewed.
- Protect patient wellness data and maintain system security and operational stability.
- Establish accountability for employees, contractors, clinical partners, and third-party service providers.

## Network Topology

This project focuses on the governance and security of Solstice Health Analytics' technology environment rather than the design of a physical network topology.

The policy applies to the systems, infrastructure, and data flows that support patient wellness services.

**Technology Environment:**

- **Cloud Infrastructure:** Systems hosted on Microsoft Azure.
- **Data Ingestion:** Wearable device ingestion pipelines.
- **Healthcare Integrations:** Telehealth integrations and clinical partner systems.
- **Internal Applications:** Applications supporting organizational operations.
- **Network Infrastructure:** Network configurations and related infrastructure.
- **Data Assets:** Patient wellness data processed, transmitted, or stored within supported systems.

**Security and Governance Controls:**

- **Access Control:** Least-privilege access, authentication, provisioning, and periodic access reviews.
- **Data Classification:** Categorization of information as Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted.
- **Remote Access Security:** Secure remote access, multi-factor authentication (MFA), endpoint checks, session controls, and VPN requirements.
- **Change Authorization:** Formal approval before implementing system or infrastructure changes.
- **Risk Assessment:** Evaluation of operational impact, security risks, scheduling conflicts, and potential disruption.
- **Rollback Planning:** Documented recovery steps in case an approved change fails.

## Configuration Steps

1. **Establish the Policy Framework**

   Identify and organize four foundational policies and two advanced policies according to their hierarchy tiers.

2. **Define Foundational Security Policies**

   Establish the following policies as the foundation of the organization's governance structure:

   - **Information Security Policy:** Defines the overall direction for protecting information and managing risk.
   - **Data Classification Policy:** Establishes information categories and handling requirements for patient wellness data.
   - **Access Control Policy:** Defines least-privilege access, authentication, provisioning, and periodic access reviews.
   - **Acceptable Use Policy:** Establishes appropriate use of corporate email, internet, and cloud systems.

3. **Define Advanced Security Policies**

   Identify two additional policies based on the organization's risk profile and operational needs:

   - **Incident Response Policy:** Covers preparation, detection, containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident review.
   - **Remote Access/VPN Policy:** Defines secure remote access requirements, MFA, endpoint checks, session controls, and VPN rules.

4. **Establish Policy Ownership and Governance**

   Assign the Head of Compliance as the Change Management Policy Owner and the CISO as the designated change approval authority. Define the applicable review frequency, approval requirements, and exception process.

5. **Create the Formal Change Request Process**

   Require every proposed change to begin with a documented Change Request submitted through the organization's change management channel.

   Each request must specify:

   - What is changing.
   - Why the change is required.
   - The expected impact.
   - The rollback plan if the change fails.
   - The affected service provider or system.

6. **Conduct Risk and Impact Assessments**

   Require the Change Advisory Board (CAB) to assess each request for:

   - Security and operational risks.
   - Business impact.
   - Scheduling conflicts.
   - Potential disruption to operations or patient wellness data.
   - Alignment with security requirements and regulatory obligations.

7. **Obtain Formal Change Approval**

   Require the CAB or designated Approving Authority to approve or reject each request before execution. Document the approval, schedule an implementation window, and obtain the required CISO sign-off before proceeding.

8. **Implement Approved Changes**

   Deploy approved changes according to the documented Change Request. Follow the approved implementation plan and ensure that rollback arrangements are available if the change fails.

9. **Conduct Post-Implementation Reviews**

   Review each implemented change to verify success, confirm system stability, compare previous and current performance, and identify financial, operational, or security impacts. Document lessons learned for future improvements.

10. **Maintain Policy Reviews and Exceptions**

    Review the policy annually or earlier when significant technology, regulatory, or operational changes occur. Document all policy exceptions and obtain formal CISO approval.

## Results and Findings

The project produced a documented policy framework and a formal change management policy designed to strengthen security governance and operational accountability at Solstice Health Analytics.

**Key Results:**

- **Policy Framework Established:** Four foundational policies and two advanced policies were identified and assigned hierarchy tiers.
- **Change Management Standardized:** A structured lifecycle was defined: Request, Assess, Approve, Implement, and Review.
- **Unauthorized Changes Addressed:** Formal change requests, documented approvals, and CISO sign-off were established as mandatory controls.
- **Risk Management Strengthened:** The CAB is responsible for assessing business impact, security risks, scheduling conflicts, and potential operational disruptions.
- **Patient Data Protection Prioritized:** Changes affecting patient wellness data and supporting technology systems must undergo risk and impact assessment.
- **Accountability Defined:** Policy ownership, approval authority, review frequency, and exception requirements were documented.
- **Continuous Improvement Supported:** Post-implementation reviews and lessons learned were incorporated into the change management lifecycle.

**Key Findings:**

- Formal change control helps reduce the risk of unauthorized system modifications and operational disruption.
- Risk assessments and rollback plans support informed decision-making and recovery planning.
- Documented approval and post-implementation reviews improve accountability and traceability.
- Annual policy reviews help maintain alignment with evolving technology, regulatory requirements, and operational processes.

**Overall Outcome:** The project provides a governance foundation for managing technology changes, protecting sensitive healthcare data, and supporting consistent security and compliance practices.
